Things to try

  1. Book a job: "I'd like a drain cleaning next week, in the morning."
  2. Ask a question: "Do you cover Round Rock?" or "Are you licensed?"
  3. Move or cancel it: give the four-character code from your confirmation email.
  4. Say something urgent (for example "smell gas"): it stops booking and routes you to a person.
  5. Ask for a time outside the hours, or one that is already taken.

Case study: the front desk that never puts a caller on hold

The problem. A small plumbing company misses calls during busy hours and after closing. Each missed call is a booking that goes to a competitor, and staff spend the day on "are you open Saturday?" questions.

What was built. A phone agent that runs on the business's own data: one file with hours, team, services, prices and FAQ. It checks the real calendar before offering a time, refuses double bookings, books, moves and cancels, sends a confirmation with a code, and routes anything urgent to a person. The emergency check runs in code, not only in the prompt, so it does not depend on the model remembering a rule.

After the call. The team gets a summary: who called, what was booked, anything urgent, and anything that failed (for example an email that bounced), so nothing is lost.

Re-use. The same engine is re-skinned for another business from a short brief (name, services, hours, prices, FAQ) in minutes; a plumber gets job addresses and "burst pipe" routing, a dental clinic gets "knocked-out tooth" routing.
